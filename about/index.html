I work from the customer problem down to the system — and back up again.

As a Director of Product, my job is to set direction: understand the market, make the trade-offs visible and give teams a clear problem worth solving. In infrastructure, that work is inseparable from the machines. Capacity, latency, failure modes, cost and operational effort are product decisions as much as engineering details.

My hands-on background is strongest in hardware and storage. The archive covers NVMe, SPDK, RDMA and the practical Linux work needed to make high-performance systems behave. I use that depth to test assumptions early and to ask better questions of both customers and engineering teams.

Local AI is the current thread. Strix Halo puts an unusual amount of memory and GPU capability into one compact x86 system. I want to understand what that unlocks as a product platform: not just which models fit, but what it costs, how it operates and whether the experience remains good under sustained use.

Soot / Silicon is where I publish the evidence behind that thinking — including the commands, versions and failures.
