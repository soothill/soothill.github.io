> **Test record:** Name the system, relevant versions, workload and the boundary that matters most. Remove this block only when the article is not reporting a test.

Open with the question, observed failure or result that made the investigation worth doing. Do not add generic background merely to create an introduction.

Explain why the answer matters to a product, operating or technical decision. Separate an external claim from what was measured directly.

## A heading specific to the first part of this investigation

Record the setup needed to understand or reproduce the result:

- hardware and device identity;
- software, model, firmware or source revision;
- launch settings and workload;
- controls, repeats and acceptance conditions.

Do not force this information into a four-item list if prose, a table or a retained data file explains it more clearly.

## What happened under the tested conditions

Report the useful measurements and observations. Include failed attempts, reversals and negative results when they changed the interpretation.

Link to retained data and primary sources where appropriate. Use exact figures only when they help the reader understand the result or make a decision.

## Where the result stops

State what the test does not prove. Name untested workloads, versions, durations or failure modes close to the conclusion they qualify.

## The decision this changed

Say what Darren would use, reject, repeat or test next. End with a bounded recommendation or open question rather than a summary of the introduction.
